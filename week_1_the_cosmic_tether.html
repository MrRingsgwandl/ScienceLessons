Core Space Terms

Tier 3 Language Calibration

Vocabulary Mastery

Orbit
The continuous, repeating mathematical curved path followed by a spacecraft or celestial body around a central source of gravity.
Astronomical Unit (AU)
A standard scientific unit of measurement equal to the average distance from the Earth to the Sun (about 150 million km). Earth is exactly 1.0 AU!
Geocentric Model
An outdated cosmological arrangement placing a static Earth at the absolute center of the universe, with stars and planets moving around it.

Connect "Geo" (Earth) to geography, and "centric" to center. Introduce AU as a spatial scaling benchmark: Earth = 1 AU. This serves as the target classification threshold on Slide 6.

The Geocentric Illusion

Why Early Models Failed

EXPLICIT TEACHING

What Our Eyes Register

Every single morning, we see the Sun rise in the east and set in the west. To an observer standing on the ground, the Earth feels solid, stationary, and quiet.

The Fallacy: Because of this personal viewpoint, early astronomers naturally concluded that the Earth was motionless and everything in the heavens circled us.

Frame of Reference:

If you are sitting on a stationary train and the carriage next to you starts moving backward, it feels like *your* train is moving forward.

How does this illusion relate to watching sunset on Earth?

Use the train analogy. Highlight that we don't feel Earth's spin because we are moving with it at a constant, unshifting velocity.

Anatomy of the Solar System

Physical Conditions & Boundaries

EXPLICIT TEACHING

Terrestrial Planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars.

Composed of heavy metals and silicate rocks. They have few or no natural moons, solid surfaces, and are placed closely together.

Asteroid Belt

The Middle Buffer Ring

A ring filled with irregular rocky debris and dust leftover from the system's early formation, separating inner and outer worlds.

Jovian Giants

Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

Huge gaseous atmospheres (hydrogen/helium) surrounding small cores. They have dozens of moons and complex ring systems.

Explain that heat from the young Sun blew light gases out to the cold outer regions, forming Gas Giants.

The Totem Tennis Analogy

Modeling Invisible Physics

Analogy Mapping

Inquisitive introduces the **Totem Tennis / Tetherball Model** to demonstrate orbital physics:

  1. The Center Pole: Acts as the massive central Sun.
  2. The Tennis Ball: Behaves as Earth moving in its curved track.
  3. The Connecting String: Acts as the invisible vector force of **Gravity**.

Have students stand up and mimic a swinging tennis ball with their arms. Emphasize how tension in the string pulls the ball inward.

Evaluating Scientific Models

Critique & Spatial Realities

CRITICAL EVALUATION

Model Strengths

  • Explains order and rotation paths clearly.
  • Condenses astronomical scales into a view that fits on a desk or screen.
  • Helps visualize the invisible vector of gravity.

Model Limitations

  • Completely distorts the real scale size of planets relative to the vast distances between them.
  • Gives a false impression that the solar system is tightly packed.

Emphasize that showing accurate sizes alongside accurate distances on a flat screen or piece of paper is impossible.

The "What-If" Physics Challenge

Inertia vs gravity

SOAR & TRANSFER

The String is Cut!

Imagine playing totem tennis and cutting the string mid-swing. The ball doesn't keep circling—it flies off in a straight line.

If the Sun instantly vanished, what would happen to Earth's orbital path?

Without the Sun's central gravitational pull, Earth's forward momentum would carry it out into deep space in a straight line.

Review the responses. Correct any misconceptions about the Earth floating or circling aimlessly; it travels in a straight line.

Analysis Question: How does a planet's distance from the Sun's gravitational mass affect its orbital speed?

Guide students to see the core trend: gravity weakens with distance, meaning outer planets need less speed to stay in orbit.
