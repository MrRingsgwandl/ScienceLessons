Core Terminology

Defining Planetary Geometry

Axial Tilt
The angle at which a planet leans away from a perfectly straight vertical line. Earth is permanently leaning at a 23.5-degree angle.
Hemisphere
Half of a sphere. The Earth is divided by the Equator into the Northern Hemisphere (top half) and the Southern Hemisphere (bottom half, where Australia is).
Equinox
Two days a year (March & Sept) when the Earth's tilt is perfectly sideways to the Sun, causing everywhere on Earth to have exactly equal 12-hour days and nights.

The 23.5° Constant

Earth is not a Wobble Board

EXPLICIT TEACHING

Locked in Space

A common mistake is thinking Earth "wobbles" back and forth during the year to change the seasons. It does not.

Earth's axis is essentially locked, pointing at the exact same distant star (Polaris) all year long.

Because it keeps this posture while walking in a massive circle around the Sun, sometimes the Northern half is pointing toward the fire, and 6 months later, the Southern half is pointing toward the fire.

The Posture Rule:

If you lean your body to the right, and walk in a circle around a campfire without changing your lean, your head will be close to the fire on one side of the circle, and far away on the other side.

Demonstrate this physically. Put a chair in the middle (Sun), lean right, and walk around it while keeping your lean locked to the right.

Direct vs. Indirect Energy

The Flashlight Analogy

EXPLICIT TEACHING

Direct Light (Summer)

When a hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, the sunlight hits the ground straight on.

  • Acts like a flashlight pointed straight down.
  • Energy is highly concentrated in a small circle.
  • Results in intense heat and higher temperatures.

Indirect Light (Winter)

When a hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun, the sunlight hits the ground at a steep slant.

  • Acts like a flashlight pointed at an angle.
  • The exact same amount of energy gets stretched over a much larger, weaker oval.
  • Results in cooler temperatures.

If you have a flashlight, shine it directly down onto a desk, then angle it. Show how the light pool spreads out and gets dimmer.

Summer in Queensland

The Tropic of Capricorn

LOCAL CONTEXT

December 21 (Summer Solstice)

On this date, the Southern Hemisphere is tilted at its absolute maximum toward the Sun.

The Sun's most direct rays hit a line called the Tropic of Capricorn, which passes right through central Queensland!

This is why our summers in Brisbane are so intensely hot and bright. The Sun is passing almost directly overhead at noon.

Connect this to their local geography. Ask them how hot the playground feels in December compared to July. It's all because of that direct overhead angle.

Orbital Milestones

Solstices and Equinoxes

EXPLICIT TEACHING

Summer Solstice

Dec 21 (Southern Hemisphere)

The day the pole is tilted closest to the Sun. We experience our longest day of sunlight and shortest night of the year.

Winter Solstice

June 21 (Southern Hemisphere)

The day the pole is tilted furthest away from the Sun. We experience our shortest day of sunlight and longest, coldest night.

The Equinoxes

March & September

Earth is "sideways" to the Sun. Neither hemisphere leans toward the star. Everywhere on Earth gets exactly 12 hours of day and 12 hours of night. (Spring & Autumn).

Equinox comes from Latin: "Equi" (Equal) and "Nox" (Night). Equal Night!

Opposite Hemispheres

Testing the Rule of Opposites

SPREAD YOUR WINGS

The Rule of Opposites

Because it is a single rigid sphere, it is physically impossible for both poles to tilt toward the Sun at the same time.

Therefore, whatever season the Northern Hemisphere is experiencing, the Southern Hemisphere MUST experience the exact opposite.
